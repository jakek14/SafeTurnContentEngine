You can be in default without missing a single payment.

Most MCA contracts include more than one event of default.

Taking a second advance without your current lender's consent? That's often a technical default event — on the first advance, not the second.

Revenue deposits dropping below a certain threshold? Some contracts define that as a breach.

Changing the bank account your lender pulls from? Usually a default trigger.

When any of these trip, the acceleration clause activates. Your entire remaining balance becomes due immediately.

If you have an MCA, pull out the agreement and find the "Events of Default" section. Count what's in there.

Most owners are surprised by what they agreed to.

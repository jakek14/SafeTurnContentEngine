Picture a roofing contractor in January. Revenue down 65% from peak. The daily MCA pull: unchanged.

They borrowed $60K in June — factor rate 1.3, about $450 a day for 8 months.

In June, that was manageable.

November through January, roofing work dries up. The pull doesn't.

$450 still clears every business day.

By February the account is short. A second advance covers the gap.

Spring: two pulls. More revenue coming — but a larger debt load than last year, stacked to get through winter.

That's not a bad-decision story. It's a product-mismatch story.

MCAs are built on fixed daily payments. Seasonal businesses run on variable revenue.

When those two structures meet, the fixed one wins.

If your revenue has seasons, your debt structure should too.

A 1.3 factor rate isn't 30% interest. Here's what it actually is.

When you take a $100K MCA at a 1.3 factor rate, you agree to repay $130K total. Not annually — total. The factor rate multiplies the amount borrowed. It doesn't adjust as you pay it down.

That $130K gets divided by the number of business days in your term. An 8-month term runs about 176 business days — daily payment is around $739. A 5-month term runs about 110 business days — same $130K, but $1,182/day.

Same advance. Same factor rate. Same total repayment.

Different daily pressure depending on how long you have.

This is why term length matters as much as the rate itself — and why the daily payment can feel very different from what you expected when you signed.

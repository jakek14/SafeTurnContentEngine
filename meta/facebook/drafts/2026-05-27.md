More revenue is not the plan. It's the delay.

Most business owners carrying MCA debt are working harder to get more cash in. The logic feels obvious — more revenue, less pressure.

But a business with daily advances has a fixed obligation leaving the account every morning regardless of what came in.

The median US small business holds 27 days of cash buffer, according to JPMorgan Chase Institute research on 600,000+ business accounts. Not 27 good days — 27 average days.

At $1,200 per day in advances, that's 27 days until one slow stretch ends the runway. A strong month refills the buffer. The same daily pull empties it again by the following one.

More revenue doesn't extend the window. It resets it temporarily.

The only thing that actually extends the buffer is changing how much leaves every morning.

That's not a revenue conversation. That's a structure conversation.

There's an industry built around "helping" businesses consolidate MCA debt. Most of it makes the problem worse.

Here's the difference that matters:

Real consolidation replaces MCA debt with a different product — monthly payments, a real interest rate, a term structure that gives the business room to breathe.

Reverse consolidation is a new, usually larger MCA that pays off your existing advances and resets the clock.

The daily pulls stop briefly, then restart — sometimes higher than before.

Total obligation increases. Factor rate applied to a larger principal. Term restarted. New personal guarantees, often.

More debt, not less. Structure unchanged.

The test: ask what loan product is being used to consolidate. If the answer involves a factor rate, it's not consolidation — it's another advance wearing a different label.

That distinction is worth understanding before you sign anything.

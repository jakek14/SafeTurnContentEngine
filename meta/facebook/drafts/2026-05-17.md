A 1.3 factor rate sounds like 30%. It isn't.

Here's what it actually is:

Borrow $100,000 at a 1.3 factor rate and you repay $130,000.

Pay it back in 6 months — your effective APR is roughly 60%.

Pay it back in 3 months — it's over 120%.

Same factor rate. Very different cost — because a factor rate doesn't account for time. The faster you repay, the more expensive the capital becomes when you annualize it.

MCA agreements aren't required by law to show you the APR. The factor rate is the only cost number most owners ever see.

Before the next advance, convert the factor rate to an annualized number. That's the real comparison to make.

Paying your MCA off early won't save you money. That's not how the math works.

With a traditional loan, paying faster means less interest accrues. The math rewards you for early payoff.

With an MCA, the total repayment was fixed the moment you signed.

If you borrowed $100K at a 1.3 factor rate, you owe $130K. Whether that takes 4 months or 8 months — the total doesn't change. Paying faster just drains cash sooner.

So if you're already stretched and you're trying to pay down your advance as aggressively as possible — you're compressing your cash flow to solve a problem that doesn't exist the way you think it does.

The lever isn't payoff speed. It's the structure.

Extending the term — spreading that $130K over more business days — reduces the daily pressure without changing what you owe. That's what restructuring actually looks like in practice.

Most owners don't know this because they're applying loan logic to a product that doesn't work like a loan.

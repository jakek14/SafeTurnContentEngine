In six states, MCA providers are required to show you the equivalent annual cost before you sign.

California, New York, Virginia, Utah, Georgia, and Connecticut have commercial financing disclosure laws that apply directly to merchant cash advances.

Before you sign, the provider has to disclose a standardized APR equivalent — not just the factor rate.

California's law, which took effect January 1 of this year, goes further: any time a provider states a rate or fee after making an offer, they have to state the APR at the same time.

Most business owners in those states have never been shown this. Either because the offer came through a broker who wasn't paying attention, or because no one asked.

If you're in one of those six states — you can ask. They're required to answer.

That number is the only real comparison to make.

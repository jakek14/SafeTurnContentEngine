Picture a business owner who got all three of their MCAs through the same broker.

First advance was for a real reason. Cash gap. Made sense.

The broker was helpful. Fast. Knew the landscape.

When the first advance was half-paid, the broker called with another option. A renewal — more capital, clear the balance, start fresh.

Second advance.

When that one got tight, same call. Third advance.

Three pulls now clearing from the account every single business day.

The broker was there every step. Always had options.

What wasn't mentioned: every advance they placed earned them a commission on closing day.

That's not unusual in MCA distribution. It's just how the structure works.

Their incentive is to place capital. Not to evaluate whether you're better served by restructuring.

Before you take the next advance — ask what the advisor earns if you borrow.

Then ask what they earn if you restructure instead.

The original funder can still work with you. The debt buyer can't.

When an MCA account falls behind and the funder can't collect, they have a choice: modify the terms or sell the account.

Funders sell charged-off accounts to third-party buyers — often at a steep discount. The buyer then pursues the full balance.

The original funder has flexibility. They can modify terms, accept a settlement, or agree to a reduced payoff — because they're still holding the paper and making the call.

Once the account is sold, the buyer owns the debt. Their model is full collection. They didn't pay a fraction to accept half.

The window to negotiate with the original funder stays open before the assignment. Once it closes, the conversation changes.

If you're behind on an MCA and haven't had a real negotiation yet — that window is still there.

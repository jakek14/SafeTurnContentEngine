Imagine a staffing company doing $2.5M a year. Profitable on paper. Out of cash every other Friday.

Clients pay net-30. Payroll is biweekly. The timing gap between delivery and payment lines up with payroll — every cycle.

First MCA: $40,000 to bridge it. At a 1.3 factor, that's $52,000 back — about $473 a day over five months.

The invoice cleared. The gap closed. The daily pull kept running.

Next cycle: same timing mismatch. Except now $473 is already leaving the account every morning before the week starts.

Second advance: $30,000. Another $355 a day.

Combined: $828 a day. Every business day. About $18,000 a month before payroll, rent, or anything else moves.

The business didn't fail. The structure started consuming the business.

The fix wasn't more revenue. It was restructuring the pull schedule before the second advance compounded the first.

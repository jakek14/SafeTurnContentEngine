Three active MCAs. One missed payment. All three in default at once.

This isn't a worst-case scenario. For businesses carrying multiple advances, it's what the contracts say.

Most MCA agreements contain a cross-default clause. The language varies, but the effect is the same: if you breach any financial obligation — miss a payment on any one of your MCAs, or in some agreements, any other financial commitment — every other lender holding that clause can immediately declare your account in default, too.

They don't have to wait for you to miss a payment with them. The breach happens somewhere else. The default happens everywhere.

For a business carrying two or three stacked advances, this changes the math significantly.

You're not managing one default situation. You're managing the possibility that one slip triggers a simultaneous cascade — three sets of collection actions, three separate escalation paths. All at the same time, from the same event.

Most business owners learn this clause exists inside an escalation, not before one.

What to do:

Pull each of your MCA agreements. Search for "event of default" in the language — specifically, look for whether a breach of any other financial obligation triggers default under that agreement. Cross-default clauses often appear in this section.

If you have multiple agreements with cross-default language, the order in which you'd address a cash flow problem may not be what you assumed.

The one you're worried about may not be the one that matters most.

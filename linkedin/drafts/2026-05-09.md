The business looks fine from the outside.

Revenue is solid. Payroll is getting made. Employees don't know anything is wrong.

But you're running the math every night before bed. Not once a month when the books close — every day. Because every day there's a pull scheduled, and some days it's closer than you'd like.

You haven't told your partner the full picture. You've given them the version that doesn't require explaining what a factor rate is.

Your accountant sees last quarter's P&L. They think things are going well.

They are, on paper.

What doesn't show on the P&L: the combined daily pulls clearing before 9 AM. The mental overhead of constantly checking — can we cover Thursday? What if Friday's deposit is a day late?

You're managing a business and a debt schedule simultaneously. One is visible to your team. The other runs entirely inside your head.

And the people around you don't have the full picture — not because you're hiding it, but because there's never a good time to explain the whole structure to someone who doesn't live in it.

That's not failure. That's what it looks like to carry it. The one whose name is on the guarantees.

The isolation is its own weight.

The pressure doesn't lift from carrying it quietly. It lifts when the structure changes.

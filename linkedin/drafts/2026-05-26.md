Most business owners measure their debt problem by the balance.

That's not the right number.

A $300,000 bank loan at $6,500 a month is a manageable obligation. You have most of the month to work with. Revenue comes in, expenses clear, and then the payment goes out.

A $100,000 MCA at $1,000 a day is a different problem entirely.

The account gets hit before the morning deposit clears. Every business day. Whether you had a strong week or a slow one. Whether payroll is tomorrow or two weeks out.

The pull doesn't respond to your cash flow. It just goes.

The pressure most business owners feel carrying MCA debt isn't about the total they owe. It's about the structure of how it leaves the account — daily, fixed, and inflexible.

That distinction matters because it changes the fix.

Reducing the balance by a few thousand dollars doesn't change the daily pressure. More revenue doesn't change it either — the pull schedule is fixed whether your deposits are up or down.

What changes the pressure is changing the structure: the daily amount, the frequency, the timeline.

That's what debt restructuring actually does. Not eliminate what's owed — change the structure of how it's repaid.

The same $100,000 obligation at $200 a day looks completely different than it does at $1,000 a day.

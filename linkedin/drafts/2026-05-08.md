You got approved. Now no one else will.

Not because your business changed. Because what your bank statements show changed.

When banks tightened their underwriting standards a few years ago, business owners did what made sense: they found capital where it was available. MCAs. Revenue-based advances. Approvals in 48 hours.

It bridged the gap. It worked in the moment.

The data since then tells a different story.

In 2021, 22% of small business owners denied financing cited existing debt as the primary reason they were turned down.

By 2024, that number was 41%.

That's not stricter standards. That's what happens when daily ACH pulls appear in 3–6 months of bank statements — and every lender who opens that file sees a business that's already over-obligated.

Revenue isn't the problem. Your credit score isn't the problem.

What's leaving the account every single morning is the problem. And it's visible to anyone who reviews your statements.

You can't fix that with a better application. You can't explain it away with a strong pitch.

The path back to clean credit access runs through restructuring what's already there — reducing the daily obligation to a level the statements can absorb.

That's not a lending conversation. That's a restructuring conversation.

And it's the one conversation that actually changes the picture.

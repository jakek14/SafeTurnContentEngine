The MCA approved you in 24 hours. A bank takes 4 weeks. Most business owners read that as: the bank is harder to work with. That's the wrong read.

Banks take that long because they're actually checking whether you can sustain the payment.

They analyze your cash flow. They model your debt service coverage ratio. They look at every existing obligation and project what another daily pull does to your working capital. If the math says you're already over-obligated — they say no.

An MCA lender checks one thing: do you have regular revenue deposits?

If yes — approved. Same day. Sometimes within the hour.

Nobody on their side checked whether the daily pull would leave you solvent after payroll.

Nobody checked whether you had other obligations already pulling from the same account.

Nobody modeled what happens to your cash position if you have a slow week six weeks in.

The approval speed is evidence of what wasn't checked — not evidence of a better product or a better fit for your business.

Here's what that means practically:

If you took an MCA and later felt like something caught you off guard — that's not because you missed something in the fine print. It's because the product was built to move fast, and fast means no one paused to ask the harder questions on your behalf.

The 24-hour "yes" isn't a feature.

It's what happens when the only question is whether there's enough revenue to pull from.

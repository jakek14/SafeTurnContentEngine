There's a clause in most MCA contracts that lets the lender take you to court — and win — before you even know there's a lawsuit.

It's called a Confession of Judgment.

You probably agreed to it when you signed. Most owners do — they're focused on getting the cash, not reading the legal language buried on page 4 of a 20-page contract.

Here's what it actually does:

If you miss a payment — or if the lender decides you've defaulted in any way they define — they can file a judgment against your business without notifying you first. Without a hearing. Without giving you any chance to respond.

The first time you find out is when your bank account is frozen.

No warning. No call. Just a restraining notice on your account.

In January 2025, New York's Attorney General canceled over 1,100 judgments filed this way — after finding the underlying MCA contracts carried effective interest rates as high as 820% per year.

These weren't fringe operations. These were established MCA providers.

New York reformed some of the COJ rules in 2019. Other states have fewer protections. And many existing agreements — especially those signed before those reforms — still contain this clause.

The Confession of Judgment isn't a technicality. It's the mechanism that lets a lender win before you can say a word.

If you're currently in an MCA, knowing what you signed matters.

You check your balance at 8am.

By 9am it's $3,000 lower.

Not because you spent it — because three lenders already did.

This is what MCA stacking feels like from the inside. You took the first advance to plug a gap. The daily payments made the gap worse. So you took a second. Then a third.

Now you have multiple withdrawals hitting before your first sale of the day, and you're wondering why more revenue doesn't seem to help.

Here's why: when 30–40% of what comes in is already committed to lenders before you can touch it, growing your top line doesn't solve the problem. A better month just means more money flows through you — not to you.

That's not a revenue problem.

That's a structure problem.

Most owners in this situation keep looking for the next source of cash. More loans, more credit, more advances. But none of that changes the underlying drain — it just adds to it.

What actually changes the situation is restructuring what you already owe. Negotiating the balances down. Consolidating multiple daily payments into something the business can sustain. Creating breathing room instead of just keeping up.

If your mornings start with watching the balance disappear before you've done anything — the problem isn't your revenue. It's what's already committed against it.

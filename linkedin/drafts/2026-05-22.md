If your exit plan from MCA debt was "get an SBA loan and pay it off" — that path officially closed in June 2025.

Most owners don't know yet.

In June 2025, the SBA updated its Standard Operating Procedure (SOP 50 10 8) with a direct prohibition: "Merchant cash advance (MCA) and factoring arrangements are not eligible for debt refinancing."

That was historically the most common planned exit for SMBs carrying high-cost MCA debt — use a low-rate government-backed loan to pay it off and start clean.

The SBA drew that line because of a pattern they kept seeing: businesses would use SBA 7(a) proceeds to retire MCA obligations, then return to the same MCA lenders within months. Default rates on those SBA loans were elevated.

So the refinancing path was closed.

What that means in practice:

MCA debt still affects SBA eligibility indirectly — those daily pulls factor into your debt service coverage calculation during any SBA underwriting, which can disqualify you from other SBA programs even for unrelated purposes.

The path forward for most businesses carrying distressed MCA debt is no longer a refinancing conversation. It's a direct restructuring and creditor negotiation conversation — working to change the obligation itself, not replace it with a different kind of debt.

Knowing what's off the table changes where to start.

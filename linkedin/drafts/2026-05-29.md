The lender calls when you're 50% paid off. Most owners think it's because they've been a good customer.

It's because the funder is about to lose their daily pull.

Here's how it works.

You took a $130,000 MCA at a 1.3 factor rate. Total payback: $169,000. You've been making daily payments for months. You're down to roughly $65,000 remaining.

The finish line is in sight.

That's when your account manager calls with a "renewal offer." You qualify for additional funding based on your repayment history, they say. They may offer to roll your remaining balance into a new, larger advance.

Most owners hear this as a compliment. A reward for being a good payer.

What's actually happening: your payoff date is approaching. The funder is about to lose a daily revenue stream. The renewal offer is how they extend it.

If you accept, here's what changes: the new advance is larger, the factor rate applies to the full new principal — not just the remaining balance — and the daily pull resets to a higher number. Six months of payments become month zero again.

And the terms rarely improve. The funder knows you've already been through one cycle. That history doesn't make you a lower-risk borrower. It makes you a proven one.

The most expensive moment in many businesses' MCA history isn't the first advance. It's the renewal.

If your funder is calling while you still owe money, understand what they're selling. They're not rewarding your payment history. They're preventing it from ending.

Picture this: A $2.8M distribution business with a motivated buyer, a signed letter of intent, and three weeks to closing.

The owner has been building toward this exit for two years. The buyer is a strategic acquirer — right fit, fair price.

Then the buyer's attorney runs standard due diligence. First stop: the state's UCC registry.

Three active filings. Two from MCA advances the owner paid off eight and fourteen months ago. One from a current advance with roughly $85K remaining.

Here's what the attorney sees: three creditors with unresolved security interests in every asset the business owns — receivables, equipment, inventory, future revenue.

The two paid-off advances? The lenders never filed UCC-3 terminations. The liens are still live on the public record.

The buyer doesn't walk away. But the terms change.

They require lien clearance as a condition of closing. The timeline stretches from three weeks to eleven. The seller chases termination paperwork from two lenders who stopped responding after the balance hit zero.

The current advance gets paid off from closing proceeds. Attorney fees accumulate on both sides.

The sale closes. The net to the seller is roughly $140K less than expected.

UCC registry searches are standard first-day due diligence. Any buyer's attorney will run them. Open liens are a public record — they don't need to be disclosed, because they'll be found.

If you're planning an exit in the next three to five years, the MCAs you take today are part of that timeline.

Your best month didn't fix anything.

Revenue was up. New clients signed. Strong numbers on the board.

But Friday rolls around and you check the account — and it looks exactly like it did six months ago. Maybe a little lower.

The money came in. It left the same day.

Not to expenses you chose. Not to inventory, payroll, or the line item you've been pushing back. To pulls you're locked into — daily ACH withdrawals from advances that were supposed to bridge a gap, months ago.

This is what the payment structure does. It doesn't register a good month. The pull happens at the same time, same amount, no matter what the deposit column looked like.

You're not failing. The business isn't broken. You're running hard, and the wins are real — they just evaporate before you can do anything with them.

And what makes it harder is that the numbers look right from the outside. P&L is fine. Revenue line is moving. Nobody would guess the account is where it's been.

That's not a revenue problem.

It's a structure problem. Every dollar that comes in gets claimed before you get to decide where it goes.

Hit a good month, look at the account, nothing moves. You've worked to hold the same floor for six months.

The fix isn't a better month. It's restructuring what's leaving — so the next good stretch can actually show up in the balance.

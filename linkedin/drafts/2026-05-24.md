You're the last person to get paid in your own business.

It doesn't feel that dramatic. You don't call it that. You just do what you have to do.

Vendors get paid on time — you need them next month. Payroll goes out on schedule — your team is counting on it. The daily pull hits when it's supposed to because there's no option; that's how it's structured.

Then you look at what's left.

Some months it's something. Some months it's less than you needed. A few months it's nothing, and you either pull from savings or call it a wash and move to the next one.

You didn't start this to take a pay cut. But here's what "making it work" often looks like from the inside: you turned yourself into the most flexible line item in your own budget. The one that adjusts when everything else is fixed.

The MCA pull doesn't flex. Payroll doesn't flex. The rent, the insurance, the vendors — none of it bends.

You do. Every month. Because you're the one who can.

That's not a failure of planning. It's what happens when one obligation in the structure becomes fixed and rigid — and everything else, including you, has to work around it.

If you've been the last one paid for longer than you planned — that's not a rough patch. That's a signal about what the structure underneath the business is actually doing.

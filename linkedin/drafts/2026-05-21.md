You saw the number. You let it ring.

Not because you were busy. Because you've had that conversation three times already — and nothing has changed.

At first, you picked up. You explained the situation honestly. You told them you were working on it, that things were moving, that you appreciated their patience. You gave them an update that was technically accurate but basically the same update as last month.

At some point, you start to feel like you're reciting a script that neither side fully believes anymore. You know the call. You know how it ends. Same question, same answer, same awkward close.

So you stop picking up.

This isn't avoidance in the reckless sense. It's what happens when someone has genuinely nothing new to offer — and they know it. The call doesn't move anything forward. It just confirms that the situation is still the same. So it doesn't happen.

The problem: avoidance doesn't stop the situation from developing. The balance grows. The options narrow. The calls keep coming, just now from different numbers.

The conversations that actually produce something are the ones where you walk in with a plan — not just an apology. A real proposal. Something to negotiate with, not just a status update.

If there's a call you've been letting go to voicemail, the answer to what you'd say might be closer than you think.

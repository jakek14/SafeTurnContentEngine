One MCA can close the door on every other lender.

Not because your revenue dropped. Not because your credit score changed.

Because of a filing that happened the moment you signed.

When you take a merchant cash advance, the lender files a UCC-1 financing statement with your state. It's a public record — visible to any lender who looks up your business name.

Most MCA agreements include a blanket lien. That means the filing covers everything: equipment, inventory, accounts receivable, future revenue, and bank accounts.

Here's what that looks like when you go to get financing elsewhere:

The underwriter pulls UCC records. They see a blanket lien. It tells them that another creditor already has first-priority claim on every asset the business owns.

That's not a risk most traditional lenders will take on — regardless of how strong your cash flow looks or how long you've been in business.

SBA 7(a) loan: blocked. Bank line of credit: blocked. Equipment financing: blocked. Not because of your business — because of a filing you signed in a 24-hour approval window.

Some lenders will overlook a lien if the MCA balance is nearly paid off. Most won't.

The path back to traditional financing starts with getting the lien released or subordinated as part of any restructuring.

That's not a credit repair conversation.

That's a restructuring conversation.

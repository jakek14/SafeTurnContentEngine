Every bank that turned you down ran the same calculation.

It's called a debt service coverage ratio. And if you're carrying MCA payments, you probably failed it — before the conversation even started.

Here's how it works:

Banks take your annual net operating income and divide it by your total annual debt service — every payment obligation added together.

Most bank lenders require that ratio to reach at least 1.25. Meaning your income has to cover your debt payments by a margin of 25% before they'll advance anything.

Now translate your MCA payments into annual figures.

$500 a day in combined pulls equals $130,000 in annual debt service.
$800 a day equals $208,000.
$1,200 a day equals $312,000.

That's just the MCA portion — before any other loans, lines of credit, or existing obligations.

A business with $400,000 in annual net operating income and $250,000 in MCA debt service has a DSCR of 1.6. Looks fine on paper.

Add a second advance. Push annual debt service to $350,000.

Now you're at 1.14. Below the threshold. Denied.

Not because your revenue dropped. Not because your credit score changed.

Because the daily pull schedule created a debt service load the ratio can't absorb — and no underwriter is going to overlook the math.

This is why restructuring the obligation — not increasing revenue — is what actually reopens bank access.

More sales doesn't move that ratio. Restructuring the denominator does.

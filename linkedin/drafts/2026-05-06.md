Your MCA lender will settle for less than you owe.

Not because they're generous. Because their math makes it work.

Most business owners assume the balance is fixed. The factor rate was set, the contract was signed, and there's nothing to negotiate.

But MCAs aren't loans — they're revenue purchase agreements. And when your business hits distress, the calculus on the other side of the table changes.

Here's what the lender is looking at when payments stop:

- Keep pulling and collect NSF fees instead of payments
- Send to collections — at 25–40% of whatever they recover
- Pursue legal action — which takes time and money with no guaranteed outcome

Or: accept a negotiated settlement. A lump sum today at a real discount.

For many MCA providers, that last option has the best economics.

That's not charity. That's math.

This doesn't happen automatically. You need to know when to open the conversation, what documentation moves the needle, and where your leverage actually sits.

But understanding that settlement is a real option — not a desperation move — changes how you see the whole situation.

Most businesses that restructure MCA debt successfully didn't get lucky.

They understood what the lender needed and gave them a reason to say yes.

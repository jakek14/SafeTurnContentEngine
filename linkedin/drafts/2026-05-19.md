There's a version of bankruptcy designed for small businesses. Most owners have never heard of it.

It's called Subchapter V — an amendment to Chapter 11, written specifically for companies with less than $7.5 million in total debt.

Here's what makes it different from standard Chapter 11:

- No creditors' committee (the main cost driver in traditional Chapter 11)
- Faster timeline — typically 90 to 180 days
- The business owner stays in control throughout
- The reorganization plan doesn't require creditor approval — it requires court approval

Chapter 11 filings rose 37% in Q1 2026 compared to the same quarter last year. Subchapter V elections specifically jumped 91% in February 2026 versus February 2025.

MCA debt is one of the significant drivers of that increase.

Most owners don't explore this option because they assume bankruptcy means the business is over. In this track, it doesn't. Subchapter V is about restructuring what's owed under court protection — while the business keeps operating.

If your debt is under $7.5 million and MCA payments are consuming working capital faster than the business can build it — this path exists. Most people in that situation don't know it does.

Knowing your options doesn't commit you to any of them.

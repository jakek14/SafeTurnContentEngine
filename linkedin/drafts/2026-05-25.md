Your MCA factor rate wasn't pulled from a hat. Here's the pricing model they used.

Most owners treat the factor rate as a given. It's actually a calculation — and the inputs to that calculation are visible in your file before the underwriter picks up the phone.

Five variables most MCA funders use:

— Bank statement health. NSFs, returned items, and large revenue swings raise the rate. Consistent deposits and a stable floor balance lower it. Every bounce in the statements tells them how often the account has gotten ahead of itself.

— Existing advances. Every active MCA is visible in your bank statements. Second-position funders are subordinated to the first advance — they price that risk into the rate. By the third advance, you're at the top of the risk tier regardless of how much revenue you generate.

— Industry type. Restaurants, retail, and construction carry statistically higher default rates and are priced accordingly. Healthcare and professional services typically price lower.

— Credit profile. MCAs don't rely on credit scoring the way banks do, but a score below 550 and any recent judgments move the rate.

— Time in business. Under 18 months is a risk premium trigger at most funders.

Factor rates typically run 1.1 to 1.5. If yours came in above 1.35, something in your file flagged — bank statement quality, industry category, or existing advance stack.

That's worth knowing.

Not because it changes what you already signed. But because understanding the inputs tells you what the funder actually saw in your file — and what would need to change before the next conversation goes differently.
